## Phase Goal

**As a** person reading the feed during an emergency, **I want to** see how many different nearby
people confirmed or disputed each report, and whether it is still current and its reporter reliable,
**so that** a few accounts cannot make a weak report look well corroborated.

<objective>
Compose a reporter's bounded history into the public reporter signal (TRUST-07, second half; slice S4
"source reliable" in the outline's MVP framing). After this plan, one pure function
`ReporterSignals(accountIDs, history, votes, now)` in a new `internal/service/reporter.go` turns the
rows that plan 03-06's `ReporterHistoryReports` returns, plus one batch of current vote rows, into a
`ReporterSignal` for every requested account (D-10): the tag, the score that stays null for a New
reporter, the everyday label, the panel sentence, the popup score sentence, and the judged and
corroborated counts (D-13, D-15, D-19, D-23, R-05, A50). It classifies each history report with
03-03's `ClassifyOutcome`, with the scored account as the reporter, and rates each account with
03-03's `ScoreReliability`, so no rule is copied. `Trust` gains its `Reporter` field, and
`TrustInput` gains a `Reporter` pointer whose nil value yields the `none` signal (A47), the real rule
for accountless reports and reports past the account cap. The signal is display only (D-14).
`docs/TRUST-MODEL.md` records the reporter half of the trust model, including the RESOLVED Q4 cap
decision and how the roadmap word "score" is met for the reporter (D-08, D-23).

Increments, task by task (each ends at something a real test observes):
- After Task 1: a test hands `ReporterSignals` real history rows and vote rows for six accounts and
  gets Reliable reporter 86, Mixed record 71, Unreliable reporter 32, the exact boundary 75, and New
  reporter with the right sentence, each with its label and plain text sentences. A reporter's own
  confirmations are proven not to count.
- After Task 2: every trust object carries a reporter signal. A test passing a composed signal into
  `BuildTrust` sees it on the trust object, a report with no rated account reads `none` with nothing
  to show, and the counts and still current signal are unchanged by it. `Nearby`, `CastVote` and
  `ActivityForAccount` carry the none signal until 03-12 sets the pointer on every read path.
- After Task 3: a reader of `docs/TRUST-MODEL.md` can see how a reporter is rated, why the read caps
  30 expired reports rather than 30 judged ones, the anchor table and invariants, and the honest
  limits of the rating. The full suite is green on a fresh disposable database `p3_0310`.

Purpose: the roadmap's second criterion asks for a slow decaying "is this source reliable" score
beside the still current one. 03-03 decided the rules and 03-06 made the history readable; this plan
is the composition that turns them into the signal 03-12 loads on every read path and 03-14 puts on
the wire.

Output: new `internal/service/reporter.go` and `internal/service/reporter_test.go`; edited
`trustcopy.go`, `trustcopy_test.go` and `trustview.go`; a new section in `docs/TRUST-MODEL.md`.
</objective>

     - The exact alternative, reading 60 and keeping the first 30 judged in Go, doubles the read cost and was not taken.
     - `ScoreReliability` applies the same cap to whatever rows it receives, neutral rows included, so the result is the same whether or not the read was capped.

5. `### Reporter score formula` (D-23):
   - Credits are 1 for corroborated, 0 for contradicted and 0.25 for unconfirmed (`UnconfirmedCredit`).
   - p is the recency weighted mean credit, with weight 2 to the power of minus the age over 30 days.
   - The score is round(100 (n p + 1) / (n + 2)) with n the judged count, so few reports are pulled toward 50.
   - Describe the computation as integer arithmetic, not as a floating point formula:

     - credits are whole hundredths (100, 0 and 25);
     - the sums are 64 bit integers;
     - the score is an integer division that rounds half up.
   - So the boundaries 75, 35 and 25 are exact (4 corroborated and 1 unconfirmed is exactly 5.25 / 7, which is 75), equal ages give equal weights, and the order of reports never changes the result.
