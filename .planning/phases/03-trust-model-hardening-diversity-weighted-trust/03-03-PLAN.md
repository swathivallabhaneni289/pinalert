## Phase Goal

**As a** person reading the feed during an emergency, **I want to** see how many different nearby
people confirmed or disputed each report, and whether it is still current and its reporter reliable,
**so that** a few accounts cannot make a weak report look well corroborated.

<objective>
This plan is the decision core of the "source reliable" half of TRUST-07 (slice S4 in the outline).
It ships two pure functions in a new `internal/service/reliability.go` and proves them test first:

1. `ClassifyOutcome` decides what one finished report says about its reporter (D-11, D-12, R-05,
   R-06): corroborated, contradicted, unconfirmed, or neutral. It asks `Resolve` itself, with
   neutralised metadata and the resolution fields zeroed, so the reporter rating can never disagree
   with the visibility ladder and self resolving can never erase a bad record (T-03-11).
2. `ScoreReliability` turns a reporter's judged outcomes into the public rating (D-13, D-15, D-23,
   R-05): credits 1, 0 and 0.25, a 30 day half life inside a 90 day window, the latest 30 rows, a
   minimum of 5 judged reports, and tags Reliable reporter, Mixed record, Unreliable reporter or New
   reporter cut from an integer score that is exact at the boundaries 75, 35 and 25.

Increment (MVP check): after Task 1 a test can hand the classifier any finished report's metadata
and tally and get the outcome the decisions define; after Task 2 a test can hand the scorer any
history and get the rating and tag. 03-10 composes these into the reporter signal on the wire, and
Phase 3.1 reuses `ClassifyOutcome` (including its neutral result) for history labels.

Purpose: the rating is public and personal (D-10), so its rules must be provable in isolation
before any SQL, handler or browser code can get them wrong. Integer arithmetic makes the tag
boundaries deterministic, which is the documented trust model reasoning this portfolio project
exists to show.

Output: `internal/service/reliability.go`, `internal/service/reliability_test.go`, two RED commits
and two GREEN commits (plus an optional refactor commit).
</objective>
