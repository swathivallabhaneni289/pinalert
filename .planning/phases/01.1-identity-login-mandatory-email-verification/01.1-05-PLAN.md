## Phase Goal

**As a** visitor, **I want to** verify my email with a magic link before I can report or vote, **so that** every report and vote is tied to an accountable, verified identity.

The loop works and the gate is closed. This plan makes the one endpoint that costs real money and real quota resistant to abuse, and gives the waiting visitor an honest, ticking answer to "when can I try again?".

<objective>
Deliver IDENT-04: verification-email requests are rate-limited per email address and per IP, with the 30–60 second resend cooldown surfaced as a live countdown.

Purpose: `POST /api/auth/request-link` is anonymous by necessity and spends a scarce free-tier quota on every call. Without a limiter it is a free spam relay pointed at arbitrary third parties and a trivial way to exhaust the quota that everyone else's login depends on.
Output: `internal/ratelimit`, the DB-backed per-email cooldown, generic 429 handling, and the check-inbox countdown.
</objective>

<decisions_resolved>
- **DEC-H — both keys, different storage (CONTEXT.md leaves the keying to Claude's discretion within IDENT-04):** the per-email cooldown is **database-backed** and the per-IP limiter is **in-memory**. This split is not arbitrary. The per-email cooldown protects a real, scarce, cross-restart resource — the provider's daily quota — and the deployment target sleeps and cold-starts, so an in-memory per-email counter would reset to zero on every wake and protect nothing. The per-IP limiter's consequence if reset is merely a briefly wider burst window, which is acceptable for an in-process map.
- **DEC-I — per-IP budget: burst 5, refill 1 token per 60 seconds.** Chosen against India-specific CGNAT collateral damage, which PROJECT.md already names as a real risk: many legitimate phones share one carrier IP, so the per-IP control is deliberately the looser, secondary one and the per-email cooldown is the primary. `middleware.RealIP` is already in the chain, so the limiter keys on the resolved client IP.
- **DEC-J — the cooldown is checked before the token row is inserted, and the insert still happens before the send.** These are two different points in one request and must not be collapsed. A rate-limit refusal never writes a row, so it cannot extend its own cooldown (UI-SPEC item 6). A send failure after a successful insert *does* consume the cooldown, so a failing or quota-exhausted provider cannot be hammered (RESEARCH.md Pitfall 3).
</decisions_resolved>
